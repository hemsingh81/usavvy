# PRD Quality Review — Usavvy

## Overall verdict
This is an unusually disciplined, thesis-driven PRD: it names a real bet ("the board, not the chat"), sequences scope around that bet, quantifies almost every NFR, and is honest about what it hasn't decided (§22, 8 open questions, explicit "before engineering commits to estimates" gate). The risk to downstream work isn't rigor, it's a few source-of-truth gaps that a chain-top document can't afford: the Glossary omits core structural nouns (Course, Module, Topic, Session) that FRs and the data model depend on, one user journey has no named protagonist, and a handful of MUST-priority requirements (cohort size cap, fair-use ceilings, minor consent flow) don't carry a testable bound. None of this is structural — it's cleanup a PM pass can close before UX/architecture lean on the document.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Trade-offs are named with what was given up, not just what was chosen. §19.1 commits to a voice-only Avatar and states the cost explicitly ("video avatars add cost, latency, and uncanny-valley risk... Every second spent on face rendering is a second not spent on NFR-B-2"), then still flags it as Assumption A2 and OQ-3 for stakeholder override — propose-and-flag done correctly, not a hedge. §7's BA recommendation R1 explicitly declines to compress cohorts into the MVP and states why ("scheduling, real-time infrastructure, and moderation complexity comparable to the entire MVP"). §21's risk table doesn't smooth ratings to neutral — R-4 (cohorts never form) and R-6 (scope: three products at once) are both rated **High/High**, not softened for optics. §22's Open Questions are genuinely open (OQ-1 monetisation, OQ-4 seed subjects, OQ-6 human-grading capacity) rather than rhetorical — OQ-6 in particular surfaces a real tension against FR-E-10/FR-E-11's MUST-priority human-review requirements ("If not, open-ended assignment scope must shrink").

## Substance over theater — strong
NFRs are quantified, not boilerplate: NFR-1 (LCP ≤2.5s), NFR-3 (5,000 concurrent sessions), NFR-5 (99.5% uptime with a stated maintenance window), NFR-B-1..6 (latency budgets in milliseconds). The Executive Summary's differentiation claim is grounded in cited learning-science mechanisms (§3.1's dual-coding row) rather than asserted novelty. Personas (§4) are the one area that brushes against the rubric's "more than four" smell — see finding below — but each of the six actually drives a specific FR, UJ, or NFR rather than sitting decoratively.

### Findings
- **low** Persona count exceeds the theater threshold but is functionally anchored (§4) — Six personas (P1–P6) is above the rubric's "more than four" smell line. Each is tied to something concrete (P1→J1/upload flow, P2→J2/pace forecast, P3→J3/cohort, P4→NFR-16 minor gating, P5→FR-O-1, P6→FR-O-4), so this reads as justified rather than padding, but worth a conscious check that P5/P6 (internal operational roles) aren't diluting the "persona" concept for downstream UX work. *Fix:* Consider relabeling P5/P6 as "internal roles" distinct from the learner-persona set, or note explicitly in §4 that only P1–P4 are UX personas.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is stated and re-stated at the point of maximum leverage: §2 ("What makes this defensible is not the chat — it is the board... everything else is scaffolding"), §10's header ("This is the product. Everything below should be treated as MUST unless marked"), and §7's MVP sequencing which puts Epic 3 (Board) in Release 1 and defers the most novel-but-unvalidated capability (cohorts, Epic 5) to Release 3 with an explicit rationale (R1). The North Star metric (§20, "Weekly Mastered Concepts per Active Learner") measures the thesis directly rather than defaulting to DAU/MAU-style activity, and several metrics carry built-in tension against gaming (Checkpoint accuracy target is explicitly bounded 60–75% "too high = too easy; too low = poor teaching").

### Findings
- **medium** No explicit counter-metrics named for the North Star or Business Objectives (§20, §3.2) — The rubric asks for named counter-metrics alongside Success Metrics. Usavvy has some organically embedded tension (e.g., checkpoint accuracy bounds, cost-per-learner-hour vs. "explain more" usage per the §16.4 BA note) but nothing is labeled as a counter-metric to the North Star (Weekly Mastered Concepts) or to BO-1/BO-2 (return rate, completion). Without one, a team under growth pressure could inflate mastery counts by softening checkpoints. *Fix:* Add an explicit counter-metric row to §20, e.g. "Checkpoint difficulty drift (should not decrease as mastery-concepts/week rises)" or reference §11.2's mastery-weighting as the enforced counter-mechanism.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
The majority of this PRD is unusually strong on done-ness: most FRs pair with acceptance criteria in Gherkin form (§8.2, §9.3, §10.5, §11.4), and soft-sounding phrases are consistently operationalized rather than left as adjectives (NFR-6's "graceful degradation" is defined as a specific fallback behavior; Principle 2's "explain differently, never louder" is enforced mechanically via FR-B-19/20's Explanation History and FR-B-21's fixed route list). That said, a handful of MUST-priority items don't carry a testable bound, and one legally sensitive flow is thin.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-G-7 cohort size cap is advisory language under a MUST priority (§12.1) — "Cohort size cap for interaction quality: recommend 6–15 learners" is tagged MUST, but "recommend" gives no enforceable bound: is 16 a defect or a judgment call? An engineer cannot write a test for this as written. *Fix:* Either state a hard bound ("cohort MUST NOT exceed 15; target range 6–15") or re-tag as SHOULD if it's genuinely a guideline.
- **medium** NFR-24 fair-use ceiling has no quantified bound (§16.4) — "Fair-use ceilings on generation-heavy actions, expressed generously and only enforced against abuse patterns" names a principle but no number, and no definition of "abuse pattern." Contrast with NFR-22/23 in the same section, which are precisely measured. *Fix:* Either specify a placeholder ceiling (e.g., "no learner encounters a ceiling below Nx median usage") with `[ASSUMPTION]` tagging pending the unit-cost model (§16.4 BA note), or explicitly defer the number to that model and say so.
- **medium** FR-A-2 minor/parental-consent flow is underspecified for its legal weight (§8.1) — The entire flow is one line: "Users under 18 enter the minor flow (NFR-16): parental email consent required before any account activity." No AC exists (unlike onboarding's AC-A-3.1/3.2), and open questions are unaddressed: what happens if the parent never responds, how is the parental email verified as belonging to an actual guardian, what does the minor see while waiting. Given DPDP/GDPR exposure and NFR-16's downstream requirements (public-leaderboard and cohort-chat exclusion), this deserves the same rigor as the adult onboarding flow. *Fix:* Add acceptance criteria for FR-A-2 (timeout/reminder behavior, fallback if consent is never granted, what UI state a gated minor sees) or explicitly `[NOTE FOR PM]` it as deferred to the Data Protection Impact Assessment listed in §1.
- **medium** Relationship between "explanation style default" (FR-A-4) and "Explanation route" (FR-B-21) is unstated — FR-A-4 lets learners set a default style (concise / detailed / example-first / analogy-first) at onboarding; FR-B-21 defines a separate 8-item route taxonomy (formal definition, worked example, analogy, visual, procedure, contrast, misconception, application) used by the re-explanation engine. The vocabulary overlaps (example-first vs. worked example; analogy-first vs. analogy) but nothing states whether the onboarding style seeds the first route choice, constrains route rotation, or is independent of it. An engineer building the Lesson Orchestration service (§19) cannot wire these together as specified. *Fix:* Add one sentence to §10.3 or §8.1 clarifying whether FR-A-4's style is the default starting route, a tie-breaker among equally-valid routes, or purely a narration-tone preference orthogonal to route selection.

## Scope honesty — strong
§3.3's Non-Goals section does real work — seven explicit exclusions, each with a one-line reason rather than a bare bullet (e.g., certification is "completion certificates only, clearly non-accredited"). De-scoping is proposed openly, not silently: §7 explicitly sequences Epic 5 and Epic 6 out of MVP with named rationale (R1), and the document's own status line ("For review — see §22 Open Decisions before engineering commits to estimates") is honest about its own readiness rather than presenting itself as green-lit. The 8 open questions in §22 are gated to specific downstream milestones ("Before UX design," "Before architecture," "Before spike"), which is the right way to surface a high open-item count on a launch-stakes document that isn't claiming to be final.

### Findings
- **low** No consolidated Assumptions Index (§4, §19.1) — Only two inline assumptions exist (A1 market/language, A2 Avatar form), and both are functionally roundtripped through §22 (OQ-2, OQ-3), so nothing is orphaned. But there's no dedicated "Assumptions Index" section as the rubric's roundtrip check expects, so a downstream reader scanning for one won't find it in one place. *Fix:* Add a two-row index table (or fold into §22) explicitly listing A1 and A2 with their §22 cross-reference, since the content already exists.

## Downstream usability — adequate
Cross-references generally resolve correctly (§9.3's "§10.2, FR-B-14," §19's "FR-B-3, AC-B-1.1" both check out), and FR/AC ID numbering is contiguous within every epic (FR-A-1..8, FR-B-1..33, FR-C-1..14, FR-G-1..19, FR-E-1..14, FR-R-1..10, FR-O-1..9, NFR-1..24 all run without gaps or duplicates). The two real gaps are the Glossary's coverage and one floating UJ — both matter more here than on a standalone PRD because this document is explicitly chain-top (UX, architecture, and epics/stories are named as the next consumers in §1).

### Findings
- **high** Glossary omits the structural nouns the rest of the PRD depends on (§23) — Course, Module, Topic, and Session are all central, repeatedly-used terms (the entire content hierarchy in FR-C-1, the data model in §18, "A Session teaches one or more Concepts" in §10.1) but none of the four appear in the Glossary, which defines Beat, Board, Avatar, Concept, Mastery, Cohort, Checkpoint, and Pace forecast but stops there. Downstream UX/architecture work that source-extracts definitions from §23 alone will not find these. *Fix:* Add Course, Module, Topic, and Session to §23 with the same one-line-definition treatment as the existing entries.
- **medium** UJ J4 has no named protagonist (§17) — J1, J2, and J3 each open with a named persona (Ananya, Ravi, Meera); J4 ("Assignment loop") opens with "Topic completes → assignment unlocked → learner reads rubric..." and never names a protagonist. This is a floating UJ per the rubric's explicit check, and it covers a non-trivial new mechanic (auto-grading, appeal path) that would benefit from carried-forward persona context (e.g., does Ananya's PDF upload flow through to this assignment loop, or is it independent of her situation). *Fix:* Reopen J4 with one of the existing personas (Ananya or Ravi both have plan/mastery context already established) rather than a generic "learner."
- **low** NFR-B-* ID namespace isn't declared in the traceability legend (§1) — §1's "Traceability ID format" states `NFR-<n>` for non-functional requirements, but §10.6 uses a distinct `NFR-B-<n>` namespace for Board performance requirements, and §16 separately uses plain `NFR-<n>`. Both are internally consistent and non-colliding, but the legend a downstream reader would check first doesn't mention the epic-scoped variant exists. *Fix:* Extend the §1 legend to note `NFR-<Epic>-<n>` is used for epic-scoped non-functionals (currently only Board/B), alongside global `NFR-<n>`.
- **low** Sections lean on section-number cross-references rather than glossary-term-only references — References like "(§10.2, FR-B-14)" and "(§11.2)" are precise but require jumping to another section rather than resolving through a shared vocabulary, so individual sections don't fully stand alone if extracted. This is a minor point given the PRD's length and internal consistency, and the cross-refs do resolve correctly. *Fix:* No action required unless sections are sharded for downstream consumption (see `bmad-shard-doc`), in which case section-number refs should be converted to stable anchors or glossary-term links.

## Shape fit — strong
This is correctly shaped for a consumer product with meaningful UX feeding downstream design and architecture: three of four external personas (P1–P3) carry named UJs (§17), FR/AC granularity matches what story-writing will need, and the confirmed brand/design system (§6) is recorded once as a shared source of truth rather than re-litigated per section — appropriate given Wireframes are named as a direct next artifact (§1) and a UX Lead is a named audience member. The one under-formalized spot is the minor/consent flow: P4 (Sameer, minors) is correctly flagged as "(secondary, gated)" but the compliance-sensitive flow behind that gate (FR-A-2) gets a single line rather than the UJ-or-AC treatment a regulated flow of this kind warrants — see the Done-ness clarity finding on FR-A-2, which applies here too.

## Mechanical notes
- **Glossary drift:** Terminology is otherwise consistent — "Board," "Beat," "Concept," "Mastery," "Cohort" are used identically across FRs, the data model, and UJs. "Learner" vs. "User" is a deliberate, not accidental, distinction (§18's data model: `User ──1:1── LearnerProfile`), not drift.
- **ID continuity:** No gaps or duplicates found in FR-A, FR-B, FR-C, FR-E, FR-G, FR-O, FR-P, FR-R, or NFR-1..24. AC IDs (AC-A-3.x, AC-C-9.x, AC-B-x.x, AC-P-x.x) correctly key off their parent FR number. The one legend gap is the undeclared `NFR-B-<n>` namespace noted under Downstream usability.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** Only two inline assumptions exist (A1 §4, A2 §19.1); both are referenced back in §22 (OQ-2, OQ-3) so nothing is orphaned, but there is no dedicated index section — see Scope honesty finding.
- **UJ protagonist naming:** J1/J2/J3 (§17) are correctly anchored to named personas carrying context inline (goals, prior state, specific numbers). J4 is a floating UJ with no protagonist — see Downstream usability finding.
- **Required sections for stakes:** For a launch-stakes, chain-top consumer PRD, all expected sections are present: problem statement, personas, principles, phased scope, detailed FRs with priorities, NFRs (performance/accessibility/security/cost), UJs, data model, architecture considerations, success metrics, risks, open decisions, and glossary. Nothing structurally required by the stakes is missing.
